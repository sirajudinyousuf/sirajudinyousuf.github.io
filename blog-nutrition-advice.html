Understanding Macronutrients

Every food you eat is made of three macronutrients. Understanding their roles is the foundation of smart nutrition:

40%
Carbohydrates
Primary energy source. Fuel for brain and muscles. Not the enemy — the wrong types are.
30%
Protein
Builds and repairs muscle. Essential for fat loss. Keeps you fuller longer.
30%
Healthy Fats
Hormonal health, brain function, and fat-soluble vitamin absorption.

The percentages above are a baseline. Our coaches adjust your macros based on your goal — fat loss, muscle gain, endurance, or maintenance.

Pre & Post Workout Nutrition

Before Your Workout (30–60 mins prior)

Your body needs fuel, not a feast. A small, easily digestible meal with carbs and some protein works best. Options: banana + peanut butter, idli + coconut chutney, oats with honey, or 2 boiled eggs.

After Your Workout (within 45 mins)

The post-workout window is critical for muscle recovery. Prioritize fast-digesting protein + moderate carbs. Options: whey protein shake + banana, chicken rice bowl, or egg white omelette + 2 slices whole grain bread.

💧 Hydration is Nutrition Too

In Avadi's heat, dehydration kills performance and fat loss. Aim for 3–4 litres of water per day. Add a pinch of pink salt and lemon for electrolytes on training days. Avoid sugary sports drinks — water and real food work better.

Nutrition Myths We Bust at Zenith Fitness

  • ❌ Myth

    Rice makes you fat and should be avoided for weight loss.

    ✓ Fact

    Rice is not the enemy — portion size and overall calorie balance is. White rice in moderation fits a healthy diet.

  • ❌ Myth

    Eating fat makes you fat.

    ✓ Fact

    Healthy fats (nuts, olive oil, avocado) are essential. Excess calories — from any source — cause fat gain.

  • ❌ Myth

    You must eat 6 small meals a day to lose weight.

    ✓ Fact

    Meal frequency matters less than total daily calories. 3 satisfying meals can work perfectly for fat loss.

  • ❌ Myth

    Protein supplements are only for bodybuilders.

    ✓ Fact

    Protein supports everyone's muscle recovery and satiety. Whey protein is safe, effective, and convenient — not just for gym rats.

Nutrition for Specific Goals

For Fat Loss

Create a moderate calorie deficit (300–500 kcal/day). Prioritize protein (1.6–2g per kg bodyweight) to preserve muscle. Reduce processed carbs and added sugars. Don't eliminate food groups.

For Muscle Building

Eat at a slight calorie surplus (200–300 kcal above maintenance). Consume 2–2.2g protein/kg bodyweight. Time carbohydrate intake around training. Prioritize sleep for anabolic hormones.

For Endurance / Marathon Running

Carbohydrates are your primary fuel. Increase complex carbs during heavy training weeks. Practice race-day nutrition (gels, bananas, electrolytes) during long training runs. Don't try anything new on race day.
