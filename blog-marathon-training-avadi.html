The 4-Phase Marathon Training Plan

A standard marathon program runs 16–20 weeks. Here's how we break it down at Zenith Fitness:

Weeks 1–4
Base Building

Easy runs 3–4x/week. Build aerobic base without strain. 20–30 km total per week.

Weeks 5–10
Mileage Build-Up

Long runs every Sunday. Weekly mileage increases 10% per week. Introduce tempo runs.

Weeks 11–14
Peak Training

Longest training runs (30–35 km). Speed intervals. Strength & mobility sessions added.

Weeks 15–16
Taper & Race Prep

Reduce mileage by 40%. Mental prep, pacing strategy, race-day nutrition finalized.

Key Training Elements We Focus On

1. Long Run Strategy

The weekly long run is the backbone of marathon training. We progressively increase distance, teaching your body to burn fat for fuel and adapt to prolonged effort. Our coaches guide every long run with pacing targets.

2. Tempo & Interval Training

Speed isn't just for sprinters. Two quality sessions per week — tempo runs at lactate threshold and interval efforts — make race pace feel comfortable on marathon day.

3. Heat Adaptation for Avadi

Training in Avadi's warm climate is actually an advantage. We schedule early morning sessions (5:30–7 AM) and teach you hydration strategies that translate into superior performance on cooler race days.

4. Strength & Injury Prevention

At Zenith Fitness, we integrate 2 strength sessions per week — focused on glutes, core, and hips — to keep you injury-free and running efficiently through all 42.2 km.

Common Mistakes Avadi Runners Make

  • Running too fast on easy days — drains your recovery and increases injury risk
  • Skipping the long run — it's non-negotiable for marathon success
  • Ignoring hydration — especially critical in Avadi's heat
  • Not doing strength work — leads to knee and IT band issues
  • Poor race-day nutrition — bonking at km 30 is avoidable with proper fueling
  • No taper — arriving at race day fatigued is a preventable mistake

Race-Day Nutrition Blueprint

Our nutrition coaches at Zenith Fitness design a race-day plan for every athlete. Key principles include carb-loading 48 hours before, a pre-race meal 3 hours out, and a gel/hydration strategy every 45 minutes during the race.

We also run practice fueling during long training runs so your gut is trained and ready — no surprises on race day.
